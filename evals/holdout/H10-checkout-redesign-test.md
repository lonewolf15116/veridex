Last month we tested a redesigned one-page checkout on TrailBay, our online outdoor gear store. Our plan was to run the test for 28 days with a 50/50 split, but we checked results daily, and on day 9 the new design pulled clearly ahead so we stopped the test and shipped it to everyone. By then 2,150 visitors had seen the old checkout and 2,130 had seen the new one. The old checkout converted 86 of its visitors, which is 4.0 percent, and the new one converted 117, or 5.5 percent. That is a relative lift of 37 percent, with a p-value of about 0.02 in a standard two-proportion test.

Day 9 happened to include a three-day weekend sale email that went out on day 6. We did not segment by traffic source, but both versions received a similar share of email traffic according to the dashboard.

Our site gets about 60,000 visitors a month. At the old 4.0 percent conversion and a $64 average order, that is 2,400 orders and $153,600 a month. At 5.5 percent it would be 3,300 orders and $211,200, so the redesign adds about $57,600 a month, or roughly $691,000 a year. On this basis we have approved a similar redesign of the product page, and we plan to credit the checkout project in our annual bonus pool.

The engineering team spent six weeks building the new checkout, and we do not plan to run a follow-up test, since the result was already significant.

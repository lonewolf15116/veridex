Brightwater Veterinary Group owns four small-animal clinics in suburban Minnesota. Combined revenue last year was $10.4M, which is $2.6M per clinic on average, and EBITDA margin was 18 percent, about $1.87M. Over the last four years revenue grew from $7.9M to $10.4M, or about 6.5 percent a year, driven by price increases of 3 to 4 percent and modest growth in visits. We have never closed a clinic and have low staff turnover, with an average veterinarian tenure of six years.

We intend to open a fifth clinic in a fast-growing suburb 14 miles from our nearest location. The lease is signed, build-out and equipment will cost $1.1M, and we will fund it from $1.5M of cash on the balance sheet without new borrowing. Based on our last two openings, a new clinic reaches about 85 percent of the mature average by its third year, which means roughly $2.2M of revenue. At our group EBITDA margin of 18 percent, that is about $400,000 a year, though new clinics usually run below the group margin for the first two years.

The main risk is recruiting. Veterinary hiring in the region is tight, and we already have an offer accepted from one associate and are in talks with a second. If we cannot staff two full-time veterinarians by opening, we will delay opening rather than run with locums. A second risk is that the new suburb has two corporate-owned competitors, which may pull on pricing, so our plan assumes no price premium over our current clinics.
